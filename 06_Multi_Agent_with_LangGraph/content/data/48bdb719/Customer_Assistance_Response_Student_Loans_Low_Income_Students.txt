#### Current Landscape

As of October 2023, the U.S. Department of Education has resumed student loan payments after a temporary freeze due to COVID-19. Current federal student loan initiatives, such as Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) plans, aim to alleviate the financial burden for low-income borrowers by capping monthly payments based on income. These plans can provide significant relief, enabling students to pursue their educational goals without overwhelming debt.

However, it's essential to note that there have been recent legal challenges impacting the implementation of certain debt relief options under schemes like the Saving on a Valuable Education (SAVE) Plan, which could affect the repayment experiences for low-income students.

#### Support for Low-Income Students

Low-income students can access federal loans which include subsidized options where the government pays the interest while the borrower is in school. For dependent undergraduates, the borrowing limits are set at $5,500 for freshmen (up to $3,500 subsidized) and increase as students progress in their studies. Independent undergraduates have slightly higher limits, providing critical funding as the cost of education rises.

Additionally, organizations such as the Louisiana Office of Student Financial Assistance and various educational advocacy groups offer resources tailored to help low-income students navigate their financial aid options and understand their rights regarding student loans.
